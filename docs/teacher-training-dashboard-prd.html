Teacher Training Project Sponsor Dashboard

Draft 0113 October 2026Web dashboardPrototype informed

A project level view for sponsors to monitor training reach, attendance, completion, batch health, and location performance, then identify where follow-up is needed.

01Overview

This product gives project sponsors and program leaders a consolidated view of teacher and student training delivery across locations. The current dashboard prototype establishes the information hierarchy and visual layout. Its figures are illustrative sample values, not connected operational data.

Product direction

Help a sponsor answer three questions quickly: Are we on track? Where are we falling behind? What needs a decision or follow-up?

Release intent: turn the dashboard prototype into a responsive, accessible, authenticated reporting experience backed by approved training data.

02Problem and goals

Problem

Training progress is spread across plans, batches, attendance records, assessments, and location reports. Sponsors need a consistent summary to compare planned reach with delivery, find operational risks, and direct support without assembling reports manually.

Goals

  • Summarize planned and actual participant progress for a selected reporting scope.
  • Make attendance, completion, certification, and batch risks easy to spot.
  • Allow sponsors to narrow results by project, geography, school, plan, batch, dates, and participant type.
  • Support investigation from an aggregate measure to relevant batch or location details.
  • Give authorized users a usable export for follow-up and reporting.

Non-goals for the initial release

  • Creating training plans or editing attendance from this dashboard.
  • Replacing the operational batch, assessment, or certificate management systems.
  • Predictive analytics or automated recommendations.

03Users and scope

Project sponsor

Reviews overall progress, compares locations, and identifies decisions or escalations needed.

Program manager

Monitors active delivery, checks delayed or low attendance batches, and coordinates follow-up.

Regional or district lead

Uses geographic filters and location comparisons to focus support in their assigned area.

Reporting administrator

Confirms reporting periods, data definitions, and access for users who need broader views.

In scope

Read-only dashboard, shared filters, KPI cards, progress and trend visualizations, batch and location summaries, attention items, role-aware access, and CSV export.

Out of scope

Source record maintenance, trainer assignment, attendance capture, assessment entry, certificate issuance, and configuration of project targets.

The dashboard supports a recurring review cycle. The sponsor narrows the reporting scope, checks progress and risk signals, then follows up with the responsible delivery team.

The labels below reflect the dashboard prototype. The product owner and data owners must approve the definitions before values are treated as authoritative.

  • All cards, charts, tables, and exports use the same applied filters and reporting period.

  • Every view displays its last successful refresh time and the applicable timezone.
Prototype data notice

The displayed figures such as 12,500 target participants, 9,850 trained participants, and 87.6% attendance are sample values used for layout. They must not be used for operational decisions.

07Success measures and quality

Product measures

  • Sponsors can identify overall progress and the highest priority delivery risks from the first dashboard view.
  • Users can apply a filter and see consistent results across every dashboard component.
  • Every attention item can be traced to the batches or locations that contribute to its count.
  • Authorized users can export a filtered report that matches the on-screen scope.
  • Metric definitions and refresh time are available without leaving the reporting context.

Acceptance criteria

  • Filter changes refresh all dependent measures and show the active scope.
  • Empty, loading, stale, and failed data states are distinguishable and accessible.
  • Tables remain usable on smaller screens and support keyboard navigation.
  • Charts include text summaries or accessible data alternatives.
  • Access control limits each user's data to their authorized projects and geography.
  • CSV exports include a generation timestamp, applied filters, and clearly named columns.

Quality requirements

  • Responsive layout supports desktop, tablet, and mobile review.
  • Use semantic headings, labeled controls, visible focus, and sufficient contrast.
  • Dashboard data should load within an agreed service target under expected project scale; the target is pending technical design.
  • Audit access to sensitive reporting data according to the organization's retention and privacy policy.

08Assumptions and open questions

QuestionWorking assumption
Which system is the source of truth?Training plan, registration, attendance, and certificate data will be provided by approved source systems or a reporting store.
Who can see cross-state and cross-project data?Access follows assigned project and geography permissions; sponsor access is explicitly granted.
How often does data refresh?Refresh cadence is set by source capability and operational need, and is visible on the dashboard.
What counts as trained or completed?Definitions are approved by program and data owners before launch.
What is the reporting calendar?Date range uses the organization's agreed timezone and calendar conventions.
Who owns each attention item?Each risk category has a named operational owner and a clear follow-up path.
Which exports are required?CSV is the initial format; export permissions and personally identifying fields are reviewed before release.
Launch gate

Confirm metric definitions, data ownership, access rules, and risk thresholds before connecting production data or presenting dashboard totals as official results.
